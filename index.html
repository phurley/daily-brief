Science & technology

AI maps the Arctic's near-freezing soil phase

A NASA-led team reports that GeoCryoAI combined 62.71 million in-situ measurements with 3.3 billion remote-sensing observations to map candidate near-freezing soil conditions across the circumpolar Arctic at 30-meter resolution. The analysis finds a longer spring zero-curtain phase and regional differences relevant to permafrost carbon feedbacks. Caveat: these are model-derived conditions, not direct continent-wide measurements of methane or carbon release; the journal labels the article an unedited version. Peer-reviewed study and NASA summary.

Milky Way merger history moves farther back

Analysis of Hubble data and globular star clusters finds evidence that a dwarf galaxy called LKH merged with the young Milky Way about 11.8 billion years ago, extending its reconstructed assembly history roughly 1.8 billion years. Caveat: this is a reconstruction from present-day evidence, not a direct observation, and the collector identified no independent replication. Peer-reviewed study and NASA announcement.

Shaded lunar niches may preserve human-associated microbes

A Science Advances study modeled several human- and spaceflight-associated microbes at the lunar south pole and concluded that shaded small-scale features could permit dormant survival for at least an Earth day, raising contamination-control concerns. Caveat: it did not demonstrate growth or replication on the Moon. NASA study summary.
